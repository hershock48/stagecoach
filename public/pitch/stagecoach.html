Section one

What we found.

Nothing here is an opinion about your design. Every line is a fact with a link, checked on August 18, 2026.

The menu, the thing people actually came for, is not on your site

Every Menu link in your navigation points at order.toasttab.com. Your own /menu page returns a 404. So the most-visited page on any restaurant website does not exist on yours: the visitor leaves for Toast's domain, and whether they come back is Toast's decision, not yours.

Your events page says you have no events

Your homepage advertises trivia Wednesdays, DJ bingo Thursdays and karaoke Sundays. Your events page, at /events, reads "There are no upcoming events." You are paying for an events calendar plugin, its stylesheets load on every page, and the page it produces tells people the busiest bar in town has nothing on.

There is no phone number on your homepage

We searched the homepage source for 781-3571 and found it zero times. It lives on the contact page, one click away, which is one click more than a hungry person on a phone will spend. Your Toast page has it above the fold. Your own site does not.

The homepage ↗

Google cannot tell that you are a restaurant

You do have structured data, the generic kind your SEO plugin writes: a web page, a website, and an organization. We read the organization block in your page source and it contains six things, of which the useful ones are a name, a logo and a URL. There is no Restaurant or BarOrPub type, no address, no telephone, no opening hours, no price range and no menu link. Search engines can read your words. They cannot read your facts, and facts are what fill in the panel beside your name.

Search engines think you have three pages

Your sitemap lists exactly three URLs: the homepage, the contact page and the privacy policy. That is the whole site as far as Google is concerned. No menu, no events, nothing to rank for "burger Marshall MI" or "trivia night near me".

Toast publishes a second Stagecoach Inn website

At thecoach.toast.site there is another site with your name, your history, your food, your Saturday live music and, notably, your phone number. It is not yours: the footer says powered by Toast and the author tag says Toast, Inc. Two sites, one restaurant, and the more complete one belongs to your ordering vendor.

Small things that add up

The homepage loads 37 stylesheets and 18 scripts. Your logo file is 284KB for a mark that displays at thumbnail size; the identical logo on the demo is 38KB. Your homepage features Ahi Tuna, which is not on the ordering menu at all. None of these is fatal. All of them are the same story.

Counted in the page source and the file headers

You are renting your whole presence, and the landlord also sells ordering.

Add it up. The site is a WordPress subscription with a page builder on top. The menu is on Toast. The ordering is on Toast. The waitlist is on Toast. The loyalty program is on Toast. There is a second website about you on Toast. If every one of those relationships ended tomorrow, what would you still have? The building, and a homepage. That is the finding. Section two

What we built.

Not a mockup. A working site with your logo, your colors, your history and your real menu in it: all 61 items, your prices, your photographs, and the actual modifier lists your kitchen uses, down to the eight dressings and the twenty-six sauce options on a burger.

A menu page that is yours

Every dish, every price, the choices spelled out, with a jump list because sixty-one items on a phone needs one. It reads from the same source the ordering system reads, so the menu people browse and the menu the kitchen sells can never drift apart.

A week that is always right

Trivia, DJ bingo, live music, karaoke, laid out as the recurring week they actually are. Nothing to enter, nothing to forget, and it can never say there are no upcoming events.

Ordering on your own website

Guests pick from your menu, choose sides and temps and sauces exactly as they do on Toast, and pay online. The order prints in the kitchen. There is a staff board for tonight's 86 list, a busy dial when the kitchen is slammed, and refunds. It costs the bar nothing a month, and we will come back to how that works.

The parts nobody sees

Structured data so Google can read your hours and address as facts. A real sitemap. Your phone number as a tap-to-call link on every screen. Zero accessibility violations at phone and desktop width, measured with the same tool the lawsuits use, not eyeballed.

And the ordering, separately

The ordering piece is its own decision and its own money, and it carries no software fee at all — nothing monthly, nothing to cancel. A 99¢ order fee is paid by the guest at checkout, the way guest fees already work everywhere online: Menufy charges $1.75, Owner.com takes 5 percent of the ticket, and Toast itself ran this exact 99 cents in 2023 and kept every penny. Ours is the one where half stays in your till.

That half is the smaller half of the story. The bigger one is that the online channel already costs you money every month, and you are probably not looking at it as a line item. So here it is as one, both ways.

Two things this dial does not hide. The first is that your guest pays 99 cents they do not pay on Toast’s page today. It is shown on the menu, before checkout, never sprung at payment. The second is that our card rate is only better on tickets above about thirty dollars — Stripe’s 30¢ per transaction is worse than Toast’s 15¢ on a small order, and their percentage is worse than ours on a big one. Processing is close to a wash. The gap in the number above is the software line you stop paying and the fifty cents you start keeping.

Where the Toast figures come from: Toast no longer publishes a fixed processing rate — it is quoted per restaurant — which is exactly why this is a range and not a number. The 3.39% figure is the $0/month Starter Kit rate, the 3.50% is the online rate on the $69/month plan, and $75 a month is what the Digital Storefront add-on is consistently reported to run. All three are worth checking, and one page of your statement beats all of it. Slide the dial anywhere you like: there is no ticket size and no Toast rate on it at which this arrangement costs the bar more than today. The worst corner of the whole range is still about forty cents an order in your favor.
